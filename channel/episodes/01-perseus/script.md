# The ENTIRE Story of Perseus — текст для записи

Серия 1 канала, 67 реплик, ~1500 слов, ≈ 10–11 мин. Читать **по репликам**: каждую 2 раза
подряд, между дублями секунда тишины. `(beat)` — пауза перед шуткой, ~0,7 с (её можно удлинить
при монтаже, укоротить нельзя). Строки в кавычках — голосом персонажа, можно утрировать.
Заголовки глав не читать: это карточки на экране.

Записывать можно частями (по главам, в разные дни): каждый файл я разберу отдельно.

## Как записывать

- iPhone: «Настройки → Приложения → Диктофон → Качество звука → Без сжатия».
- Маленькая комната с мягкими вещами, телефон на опоре в 15–20 см, чуть сбоку, авиарежим.
- В начале каждого файла 5 секунд тишины (по ней чистится шум).
- Передать через AirDrop или «Сохранить в Файлы»; Telegram — только «Отправить как файл».
- Сбился — просто повтори реплику целиком, лишнее вырежу.

## Текст

### Холодный старт

**1.** This is Perseus. (beat) By the end of this video, he will cut off a monster's head,
turn a whole wedding into statues, turn a giant into a mountain, and kill his own grandpa.
(beat) With sports.

**2.** And the craziest part? (beat) He is one of the good guys.

**3.** This is the entire story of Perseus.

### Chapter 1. The Prophecy

**4.** Our story starts in the Greek city of Argos, with King Acrisius. Acrisius had a
daughter, Danaë. But he wanted a son. So he went to the Oracle of Delphi. Basically ancient
Google, but with more smoke.

**5.** He asked: "Will I have a son?" The Oracle said: "No. (beat) But your daughter will.
And your grandson will kill you."

**6.** Now, a normal person would say: "Okay. I will be really, really nice to my grandson."
(beat) Acrisius did not say that.

**7.** He built a bronze tower and locked Danaë inside. No doors. No boys. No visitors. No
grandson. (beat) Problem solved.

**8.** Problem not solved. Because this is Greek mythology, and in Greek mythology, there is
always Zeus.

**9.** Zeus saw Danaë, liked Danaë, and got into the tower... as a shower of golden rain.
(beat) Don't ask. Nobody knows. The Greeks didn't know either.

**10.** Nine months later: baby Perseus. (beat) Already wearing a helmet. Probably.

### Chapter 2. The Box

**11.** Acrisius heard a baby crying in the tower that was supposed to have zero babies. He
was not happy.

**12.** He couldn't just kill them. Killing your own family made the gods very angry. So he
did the next best thing. He put Danaë and the baby in a wooden box... and threw the box into
the sea. (beat) Totally different. The gods can't say anything.

**13.** The box floated for days, and finally washed up on the island of Seriphos.

**14.** There, a fisherman named Dictys pulled it out of the water, opened it, and found a
woman and a baby inside. (beat) Best catch of his life.

**15.** Dictys was kind. He took them home and raised Perseus like his own son. Remember
Dictys. He is the only normal person in this entire story.

### Chapter 3. The Party

**16.** Years passed. Perseus grew up strong, brave, and... let's say, very confident.

**17.** Unfortunately, Dictys had a brother: Polydectes, the king of the island. And
Polydectes wanted to marry Danaë. Danaë said no. Perseus said no. Polydectes heard "maybe".

**18.** So he made a plan. He threw a big royal party and told everyone: "I'm getting
married! Bring me a gift. A horse would be nice."

**19.** Every guest brought a horse. Perseus had no horse. Perseus had no money. Perseus
had... confidence.

**20.** "Horses are boring! Name anything, and I will bring it. Even the head of Medusa!"

**21.** (beat) The room went quiet. The king smiled. "Deal."

**22.** And that is how Perseus signed up to die. At a party. With no plan. In front of
everyone.

### Chapter 4. Help from Above

**23.** Medusa was a Gorgon: a monster with snakes for hair. One look at her face, and you
turned into stone. Heroes had tried before. Her cave was full of very surprised statues.

**24.** But the gods were watching. Athena, goddess of wisdom, and Hermes, the messenger
god, decided to help. Mostly because Perseus was Zeus's son. (beat) Nepotism: also a Greek
invention.

**25.** Athena gave him a bronze shield, polished like a mirror. "Don't look at Medusa. Look
at her reflection." Hermes gave him a sword that could cut anything, and flying sandals.

**26.** There was one problem. Nobody knew where Medusa lived. Nobody... except the Graeae.

### Chapter 5. The Grey Sisters

**27.** The Graeae were three old sisters. They were born old. Grey hair from day one.

**28.** And between the three of them, they had one eye. (beat) And one tooth.

**29.** They passed the eye around and took turns seeing. "My turn!" "No, my turn!" "You had
it yesterday!"

**30.** Perseus waited for the moment the eye was in the air... and grabbed it.

**31.** "Who took the eye?" "I don't know, I can't see!" "Nobody can see!"

**32.** Perseus made a simple offer: "Tell me where to find the nymphs who can help me, and
you get your eye back." (beat) They told him everything. Very fast.

**33.** The nymphs gave him a helmet of invisibility, which is amazing. And a bag. (beat) Just
a bag. A really good bag. You can't carry Medusa's head in a normal bag. Trust me.

### Chapter 6. Medusa

**34.** With his new gear, Perseus flew to the edge of the world, to the cave of the
Gorgons.

**35.** There were three Gorgon sisters. Two of them were immortal. Medusa was not. (beat)
So, good news: only one of them could die.

**36.** The Gorgons were asleep. Perseus walked in backwards, looking only into his shield,
past the statues of all the heroes who did look.

**37.** One swing of the sword... and Medusa's head was off. (beat) While she was asleep.
(beat) Truly, a hero.

**38.** And then, out of Medusa's neck, jumped a winged horse. Pegasus. (beat) Greek
mythology does not explain this. Neither will I.

**39.** The other two Gorgons woke up, saw their sister, and they were not happy. But Perseus
put on his helmet, became invisible, and flew away. With the head. In the really good bag.

### Chapter 7. The Way Home

**40.** Now Perseus owned the deadliest weapon in Greece. The instructions were simple.
(beat) Don't look.

**41.** On the way home, he stopped at the land of the giant Atlas, the guy who holds up the
sky. Perseus asked to rest for the night. Atlas said no. Rudely.

**42.** So Perseus showed him the head. (beat) Today, Atlas is a mountain range in Morocco.
Be nice to tired travellers.

**43.** Then, flying over Ethiopia, Perseus saw a princess chained to a rock by the sea.

**44.** Her name was Andromeda. Her mother, Queen Cassiopeia, had bragged that her daughter
was more beautiful than the sea nymphs. The sea god Poseidon heard this and sent a giant sea
monster to destroy the kingdom.

**45.** The only way to stop it: give Andromeda to the monster. As a snack. (beat) Thanks,
mom.

**46.** The monster came out of the water. Perseus came out of the sky. Perseus showed the
monster the head. (beat) Problem solved.

**47.** Andromeda's father, the king, was so happy, he let Perseus marry her right away.

**48.** Small problem: Andromeda already had a fiancé. Her uncle, Phineus. Yes. Her uncle.
(beat) Greek mythology, everybody.

**49.** Phineus burst into the wedding with an army. "She is mine!" Perseus said: "Everybody
on my side... close your eyes."

**50.** (beat) It was a very quiet wedding after that. But the decorations were amazing.

### Chapter 8. Payback

**51.** Perseus came back to Seriphos with a wife and a head in a bag. And he found his
mother hiding in a temple, because Polydectes was still trying to marry her.

**52.** Perseus went straight to the palace. The king was having another party, of course.
He laughed: "Look who's back! Where's my gift?"

**53.** "You wanted the head? (beat) Here's the head."

**54.** Polydectes and all his friends became statues. (beat) Very quiet party after that.

**55.** Perseus made Dictys, the nice fisherman, the new king. The only normal person in the
story got a crown. Good for him.

**56.** Then Perseus gave the magic gifts back, and gave Medusa's head to Athena. She put it
on her shield. Forever. Because Athena is a little scary too.

### Chapter 9. The Discus

**57.** Finally, Perseus went home to Argos to meet his grandpa. He wasn't angry. He just
wanted to say hi.

**58.** But Acrisius heard that his grandson was coming, remembered the prophecy, and ran
away to another city.

**59.** Far away from Perseus. Safe. (beat) Probably.

**60.** Years later, that city held big sports games. Perseus came to compete. Acrisius came
to watch.

**61.** Perseus threw a discus. The wind caught it... and it hit an old man in the crowd.
(beat) Grandpa.

**62.** The oracle was right. You can't run from fate. (beat) Fate has very good aim.

### Финал

**63.** Perseus felt so bad, he didn't even want to be king of Argos. He traded it for
another kingdom and built a new city there: Mycenae, one of the greatest cities of ancient
Greece.

**64.** And when they all died, the gods put them in the sky as stars. Perseus, Andromeda,
Cassiopeia, the king... even the sea monster. (beat) Participation award.

**65.** Cassiopeia got stars too. But she spends half the year upside down. For the bragging.

**66.** So that's the story of Perseus: a hero who won with a mirror, a bag, and a lot of
luck.

**67.** Subscribe for more myths. Or don't. (beat) Just... don't look.
